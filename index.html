CareerCloset

Professional clothing should open doors, not close wallets.

A campus-based rental pilot for interviews and career events

Funding decision: $15,000 to prove one repeatable, paid campus pilot.

0:25–1:10 · Customer problem + evidence

Career opportunities have a dress code. Access to the clothes is uneven.

Customer hypothesis

Students preparing for interviews, career fairs, and presentations need professional clothing without buying a full wardrobe.

Evidence boundary

This package contains no CareerCloset interviews, paid rentals, or measured demand. The need is a hypothesis, not traction.

First evidence target: 30 student interviews that test event frequency, current alternatives, fit needs, and willingness to pay.

1:10–1:50 · Product

Reserve an outfit for the event, then return it nearby.

    Scheduled campus windows

Pilot operations: curated inventory, documented condition at pickup, deposit/card authorization, cleaning, and tracked return windows.

1:50–2:25 · Beachhead + validation

Start with one campus and one trusted distribution partner.

Beachhead

One assumed 10,000-student campus with recurring interview and career-fair demand.

Reach

Career center first; student organizations and employer partners extend the pilot.

What we need to learn

Which sizes turn, what students will pay, whether fit works, and whether returns are reliable.

Campus size and channel access are planning assumptions, not sourced market measurements or confirmed partnerships.

6:40–7:25 · Risks + financing fit

We are financing the risks that the pilot can actually resolve.

Operating risks

Fit gaps, low utilization, damage, late returns, and cleaning cost can erase the assumed $21 contribution.

Why this financing

Debt needs predictable cash flow; a larger equity raise is premature. A small proposed SAFE funds a bounded test with explicit stop/go gates.

Controls to test: deposits or card authorization, condition records, short rental windows, and return reminders.

7:25–8:00 · Founder objective + close

Our objective is affordable access, earned through disciplined proof.

Fund one campus. Measure every rental. Expand only when access and unit economics both work.

We are asking for $15,000 to turn a promising customer hypothesis into a paid, measurable campus pilot.
